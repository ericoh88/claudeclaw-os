# Don't Waste 2026 on the Wrong Career (ML vs AI Engineer)

**Date:** 2026-04-30
**Channel:** Zen van Riel
**URL:** https://youtube.com/watch?v=cqDQV5g7zHo
**Duration:** 05:37

## Summary

- Zen argues that many aspiring engineers waste time trying to pursue ML engineering and AI engineering simultaneously, not realizing they are fundamentally different roles with different skill requirements and day-to-day work.
- **AI engineers** integrate existing models into applications (RAG systems, LLM pipelines, inference APIs) — the focus is software and data engineering, not math or training from scratch.
- **ML engineers** train models from scratch and require deep knowledge of math, statistics, and data science — they compete against PhDs and face a steep self-teaching curve.
- AI engineering is framed as more accessible and self-teachable (following the same proven path as traditional software engineering), and is currently booming due to LLMs being applicable across virtually any industry.
- Zen pitches a free "Super AI Transcript" starter project (voice recorder + LLM cleanup tool) as an example of a real AI engineering project, and plugs his community for aspiring AI engineers.

## Key Points

1. **The core distinction:** AI engineers use models as tools; ML engineers build the models. The former is software+data engineering; the latter is research+math. Conflating the two is where most beginners get stuck.
2. **AI engineer scope:** Integrate existing models (not necessarily LLMs), build systems like RAG over company databases or review aggregation pipelines. Fine-tuning is the extreme edge case, not the norm.
3. **ML engineer scope:** Training pipelines, validation/test sets, model bias testing — data engineering for training, not production inference. Competing against PhD-level candidates in a more academic market.
4. **Self-teach viability:** AI engineering has a proven self-teaching pathway (same as traditional SWE). ML engineering has brutal competition without formal academic credentials.
5. **Day-to-day difference:** ML engineers test model bias in training; AI engineers run A/B tests in production to improve user experience. One is theoretical optimization; the other is shipping and iterating.
6. **Future-proofing:** Even if AI becomes as powerful as boosters claim, someone needs to integrate, configure, and deploy models at the infrastructure/application/Python layer. AI engineers remain necessary.
7. **Starter project — Super AI Transcript:** Browser API for recording → Python FastAPI backend → local Whisper for transcription → local LLM to strip filler words. Full-stack but explainable in one sentence for interviews.
