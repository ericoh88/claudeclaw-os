## Summary

- Defines the "product engineer" role: a single person who owns product decisions, design, and code -- replacing the traditional PM + designer + engineer triad at startups
- Makes the case that this is an emerging high-demand role (~57,000 LinkedIn listings) concentrated in fast-moving startups and scaleups, not FAANG
- Explains what separates product engineers from regular devs: customer empathy, broad tool knowledge, direct user contact, and being measured on real user outcomes rather than code quality
- Introduces "AI native product engineer" as the next evolution -- product sense + full-stack + LLM integration = unfireable
- Closes with a 3-step practical path into the role: ship real projects, talk to users, learn product analytics

## Key Points

1. **What a product engineer is**: Sits at the intersection of software engineering and product management. They originate ideas, talk directly to customers, make product decisions, and ship the code -- or ship on a small team. Not a PM, not just a developer. Owns the entire loop.

2. **Why startups invented this**: Small teams can't afford the back-and-forth of three separate specialists. Turns out engineers with product sense move faster than siloed teams. James Hawkins (PostHog CEO): "Engineers who own product decisions ship faster and better."

3. **Measurement model is different**: Traditional SWE = measured on code quality. PM = measured on business metrics. Product engineer = measured on real user outcomes. Whether users actually use what you built.

4. **Job market reality**:
   - 57,000+ listings on LinkedIn (US)
   - Levels.fyi doesn't even track it as a distinct category yet -- early trend
   - ~75% of listings require 3+ years experience -- no real entry level
   - Concentrated in startups/scaleups; Google/Meta/Amazon/Apple don't use the title

5. **Companies leading this model**: PostHog (entire eng org structured this way, engineers do customer support rotations, write docs, own features end-to-end), Vercel, Intercom (30k customers, 1500 employees), RAMP (already hiring AI product engineers at all levels)

6. **What separates them from regular devs** (per Lee Robinson, Vercel): You don't need to be a world-class engineer. You need broad knowledge of tools and deep applied experience. Customer empathy tops every list. You talk directly to users -- not through a PM relay.

7. **AI coding tools lower the barrier for PMs**: Cursor, GitHub Copilot, Claude Code -- if you're coming from a product background these make the technical learning curve manageable. You won't vibe-code everything, but it gives you an edge.

8. **AI native product engineer** -- the emerging hybrid: product sense + full-stack + ability to integrate AI/LLM models. You design which AI capabilities to build, talk to users about their AI needs, then ship the solution yourself. PostHog already has explicit "AI product engineer" roles building LLM features into their analytics platform.

9. **The "unfireable combo"**: Product sense + full-stack skills + AI model integration. You're not just building AI features someone else designed -- you're deciding what to build, validating with users, and shipping it.

10. **How to break in** (3 steps):
    - Build side projects that solve real problems (not tutorial projects), ship them publicly
    - Practice talking to users about their needs -- a skill most engineers never develop
    - Learn product analytics (PostHog, Amplitude, Mixpanel) -- understand how users interact with your features and measure whether things actually work
    - Best credential: a portfolio of products with evidence of real users

11. **Most common transition path**: Software engineering → product engineer (not PM → product engineer). Engineers who get frustrated with product decisions develop product sense on their own, then get the opportunity to own it.
