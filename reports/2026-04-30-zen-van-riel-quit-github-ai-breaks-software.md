## Summary

- Zen van Riel resigned from GitHub (senior engineer, ~4 years) to work on AI safety at a research lab
- His core concern: companies are handing entire SDLC stages (code review, testing, deployment, architecture) to AI agents with shrinking human oversight
- The "100x developer" paradox: if AI generates 100x the code, no human can meaningfully review it — and the industry's answer is "use more agents to review agent output"
- He calls this a mathematical guarantee of bugs, not a gut feeling — imperfect systems stacked on imperfect systems don't converge to perfection
- He's not anti-AI coding — vibe coding and productivity boosts are fine for most software — but critical systems (healthcare, finance, infrastructure) are the danger zone

## Key Points

1. **GitHub was genuinely a great job.** He built the first version of AI support systems there, helped other engineers build LLM solutions, and reached senior engineer in 4 years. He's not leaving out of bitterness.

2. **The trend he observed:** Companies aren't just using AI for code generation anymore. Review, testing, deployment decisions, and architectural choices are being handed to agents with less and less human oversight. This includes companies with millions of users, not just risk-tolerant startups.

3. **Pull requests called a "bottleneck."** Some organizations are starting to frame PR review — the last human checkpoint before production — as a slowdown to eliminate. The end goal is fully autonomous software development.

4. **The 100x paradox.** Executives love the idea of every dev being a "100x developer." But if you generate 100x the code, you can no longer meaningfully review what was produced. Rather than seeing this as a problem, the industry's response is to have agents review agent output.

5. **Stacking imperfect systems.** AI writes better code *often*, but not always — it hallucinates, makes mistakes, produces incomplete solutions for complex tasks. And it always produces far more code. An imperfect system generating enormous output creates a statistical guarantee of bugs humans can't catch. The industry's fix: more agents with slightly different system prompts. He calls this "playing dollhouse."

6. **The direction is intentional.** Not rogue AI — companies are actively, strategically choosing to prioritize raw code output over quality oversight.

7. **His confident prediction:** Full autonomous SDLC deployment will lead to an absolute increase in broken software.

8. **Nuance on vibe coding:** He's progressive about AI coding. Some quality drop is acceptable. Shipping more and breaking small things is often a net win. Non-programmers getting into software is great. The problem is *pervasiveness* — agents spreading into healthcare, finance, infrastructure where bugs cause real harm.

9. **What he's doing about it:** Joined a research lab building monitoring systems specifically designed for AI coding agents. Will share full details on LinkedIn.

10. **Career message:** 4 years ago he was a junior. He's now building something he thinks genuinely matters for the industry — a high-paid career can coexist with meaningful work.
